# CK-03-09 integration boundary — orchestrator design

The accepted training fixture exports a bounded Grid, not a generated RoleFloorPlan. Keep the native diagnostic world coherent: its rendered occupancy, player collision and melee LOS must all use that same Grid. Do not disguise it as a validated procedural campaign floor.

Add a development/test-only diagnostic-world installation path to FloorSession and PlayerSession. FloorSession owns replacement/retirement of the diagnostic rendered room, cancels superseded loads and retains the existing fixed clock/lifecycle. Its diagnostic snapshot reports diagnostic mode and the actual Grid dimensions/occupancy; generated floor, seed, hash and role metadata are absent for this mode. Normal generated loading retains its current contracts and replaces the diagnostic world cleanly. Production cannot install this world. A guarded dynamic import and visibly labeled development training controls keep training content out of normal production composition.

PlayerSession accepts the diagnostic Grid and an explicitly validated starting pose. Widen the pure pose-clearance check to Grid, and add a Grid-based state initializer sharing the existing resource constants/creation. Preserve generated-entry repair behavior and all current movement/capture/dash/sprint tests. Both modes use the same native controller, input sampler and fixed simulation path.

Introduce a narrow combat step hook in PlayerSession. After look and movement/dash/sprint costs, before the one resource-regeneration call, pass live PlayerState, GameCommand, shared ResourceRegenTimers, fixed dt and the actual tick to CombatSession. Adopt the returned resources/timers and combine movement and weapon spend flags. This applies to both the walking and dash branches. Weapon release cannot benefit from same-tick regeneration before paying its cost. For catch-up step i, the actual tick is the FloorSession final tick minus step count plus i plus one.

CombatSession owns sword state/runtime, physical damage resolver, actor records and combat event collector. Training fixtures provide initial actor/grid/pose data; CombatSession becomes the authoritative live actor-health store. Damage adopts immutable resolver results into those records, and actual fixed-tick events drive feedback. Queries use the live player pose and current living colliders, rather than the fixture's fixed setup query or per-button event counter. Normal M03 generated rooms have no enemies yet; M04 adds them later. Runtime IDs remain unique across resets.

Cancel sword anticipation/attacks immediately on loss of capture, pause, blur, hidden state, editable focus, world load/replacement and teardown, including frames with zero simulation steps. Cancellation synthesizes no release and preserves committed costs. Clear stale input before processing new valid edges. Update the scene-owned sword viewmodel after camera interpolation each frame, and update HUD from actual player resources and sword selectors. Pause freezes feedback timers; teardown removes owned subscriptions, DOM and GPU resources.

Provide native browser checks through the real development app: trusted pointer capture plus tap/release and full hold/release produce 18 and 54 damage, a wall-blocked swing misses while paying its committed cost, and pausing during charge causes neither damage nor a release on resume. Read-only diagnostics may expose detached combat snapshots; no production mutation globals or direct-damage shortcuts establish these tests. Preserve existing production and M02 browser regressions. Inspect an integrated combat screenshot and record the exact hosted source/run and test limitations.
